The five rules of the DTA sales team

  1. Every lead goes into the DTA Sales app the same day — if it is not in the app, it does not exist.
  2. Understand before you pitch. Ask first, then show only what solves their problem.
  3. Demo everything that is serious. A demo, not a brochure, closes deals. Request it from the DTA team in the app.
  4. Follow up on schedule. Most deals are won on the 3rd–6th contact, not the first.
  5. Promise only what we deliver. Honest delivery dates, honest features, clear price. Happy clients renew and refer.

4. Qualify, then pitch

The five qualifying questions

QuestionExample
Who decides?“Besides you, who else will be part of choosing this?”
What do you use today?“How do you handle billing / stock / customers right now?”
What is the pain?“What takes the most time or causes the most mistakes?”
Budget?“What have you spent on this before, or what feels reasonable?”
When?“When would you like it running?” (If “someday”, schedule a later follow-up.)

If there is no pain and no decision-maker after two honest conversations, move the lead to a long follow-up (30–60 days) and spend your time on better prospects.

Pitch by product line

Software DTA to fill: product names

Pain: manual bills, mistakes, no clear picture of stock, sales and dues.
Pitch: “Run your billing and accounts faster and with fewer errors, and know where you stand every day.”
Show: create a bill, check a report, find an outstanding payment — in under 5 minutes.
Offer: subscription (monthly / yearly) with support and onboarding.

Applications

Pain: orders and bookings by phone, no way to reach customers directly.
Pitch: “Give your customers an easy way to order or book — and give yourself a record of everything.”
Show: a similar app flow (sample or screenshots).
Offer: scoped build with clear milestones, delivery date and maintenance plan.

Websites

Pain: customers can’t find the business online; competitors look more professional.
Pitch: “Be found on Google and look trustworthy before the customer even calls.”
Show: live samples in their own segment.
Offer: design + hosting + maintenance package; online store as an upgrade.

Bundles & upsell

Software + website, or website + app, is easier to justify than separate buys. After a software client is stable (30–60 days), offer a website or app. Pricing for bundles is set by DTA management DTA to fill.

5. The demo playbook

A demo is where deals are won. Use the Request a demo button in the DTA Sales app — the request reaches the DTA admin team, who schedule it and message you the date and meeting details.

BeforeDo this
Qualify firstRequest demos only for leads with a real problem and a decision-maker present.
Brief the teamIn the request notes: what the client does, their pain, what to focus on, who will attend.
ConfirmSend the client date, time, link or address, and who from DTA will attend. Reconfirm the day before.

The 25-minute structure

  1. 2 min — Agenda: “I’ll show how we solve the problem you described, then we’ll talk about next steps.”
  2. 3 min — Their problem: repeat it in their words.
  3. 12 min — Show the solution: only the 3 features that solve their pain, using their kind of data.
  4. 5 min — Questions & objections: answer honestly; if you don’t know, say you’ll confirm within 24 hours.
  5. 3 min — Next step: “Shall we start next week? What do you need to get it approved?” Fix a date for the decision.
After the demo (within 24 hours): send a short summary on WhatsApp / email with the offer, price, start date and the agreed decision date. Update the stage in the app and set the follow-up date.

Sample messages

First call: “Namaste, I’m ___ from DTA in ___. We help businesses like yours ___ (save time on billing / get more customers online). Can I take two minutes to ask how you handle this today?”
After demo: “Thank you for your time today. As discussed, here is the summary: ___ . Offer: ___ . Start date: ___ . Shall I confirm for ___ ?”
Follow-up: “Hi ___, just checking if you had a chance to discuss with your partner. Happy to answer any question — would Thursday 4 pm work for a quick call?”

6. Handling objections

Listen fully, agree with the concern, answer with facts, then ask for the next step. Never argue.

They sayYou can answer
“It is too expensive.”“I understand. What matters is what it saves — if it saves X hours and avoids mistakes each month, it pays for itself. Let’s compare the monthly cost with the time and errors you have today.” Offer the monthly plan before discounts.
“We already use Excel / another software.”“Good — then you know what you need. What is the one thing it does not do well? Let me show how we handle that one thing.”
“My staff won’t learn it.”“We train your team and support them after the start. We’ll begin with just the daily tasks.”
“Is my data safe?”Explain how data is stored and backed up, who can access it, and how access is controlled DTA to fill: confirm the facts. Never promise what you cannot confirm.
“I need to think / ask my partner.”“Of course. What would you want to be clear on before deciding? Can we speak again on ___ with your partner joining?”
“Send me details on WhatsApp.”Send a short, specific message — not a brochure — and fix a call time: “I’ll call you Thursday at 5 to answer questions.”
“A local person will do it cheaper.”“Cheaper can cost more later. We offer ongoing support, updates and a company behind you. Let me show you what happens after the sale.”
“Not now — maybe later.”Find the real reason, agree a date to talk again, and set it in the app. Stay useful (share a tip or a case study).

Pricing and discount principles

  • Use the price list set by DTA management DTA to fill. Do not invent prices in the field.
  • Sell value first, discount last. If a discount is allowed, trade it for something — a yearly plan, advance payment, or a referral.
  • Never discount more than the limit set by management without approval.
  • Put the scope, price, payment terms and start date in writing before work starts.

Do and don’t

Do

  • Log every lead and activity the same day.
  • Ask questions before pitching.
  • Be on time for demos and calls.
  • Follow up on the dates you set.
  • Be honest about features and timelines.
  • Ask happy clients for referrals.

Don’t

  • Don’t promise features or dates you can’t confirm.
  • Don’t quote prices or discounts outside the approved list.
  • Don’t share your login with anyone.
  • Don’t criticise competitors; show your value instead.
  • Don’t leave leads without a next follow-up.
  • Don’t ignore a client after the sale.
Remember: the best sales team is the one clients trust. Be helpful, be consistent, and keep the data in the app up to date — that is how DTA grows across India.
