Stage 1 · Week 1 — Get ready

Goal: be confident with the app, the products and your pitch before you meet your first customer.

DayWhat to do
Day 1Get your login ID and password from the DTA admin (only the admin can create them). Install the DTA Sales app and log in. Keep your password private — never share it.
Day 2Learn the app: add a practice lead, move it through the stages, log a call, set a follow-up date. Open Demo requests and Monthly business to see where things appear.
Day 3Learn the products. Your manager will share the product list and price list — use only those. For each product write: who needs it, what problem it solves, one key benefit.
Day 4Watch or attend a demo of each product. Practise your 30-second introduction out loud (see the box below).
Day 5Build your first target list of 30 businesses in your city: shops, offices and clinics near you that handle many bills, stock or customers. Plan your first week of visits.
Your 30-second introduction:
“Namaste, I’m ___ from DTA in ___. We help businesses like yours ___ (manage billing and accounts faster / get more customers online / take orders through an app). May I ask how you handle this today?”

How a first visit or call should go

  1. Greet and introduce yourself and DTA (use your 30-second introduction).
  2. Ask, don’t pitch: how do they handle billing / stock / customers today? What takes most time or causes mistakes?
  3. Listen and note their answers in the lead’s Notes.
  4. Offer one next step: a short demo, or a second call. Don’t try to sell everything at once.
  5. Set the follow-up date before you leave — and log the visit.
Qualify with five questions: Who decides? What do they use today? What is the pain? What is the budget? When do they want it? Only request a demo for leads that have a real problem and a decision-maker.

Stage 3 · Months 2–3 — First clients

Goal: turn demos into clients and build a steady weekly routine.

Getting a demo (it is where deals are won)

  1. In the app, open the lead and press Request a demo (or go to Demo requests → Request a demo). Add what the client does, their problem, and who will attend.
  2. The DTA team schedules it and sends you the date and meeting details — you will see it in Demo requests.
  3. Confirm the time with your client, and again the day before.

The 25-minute demo structure

TimeWhat to do
2 minAgenda: “I’ll show how we solve the problem you told me about.”
3 minRepeat their problem in their own words.
12 minShow only the 3 features that solve that problem.
5 minQuestions and objections — answer honestly; if unsure, say you will confirm within 24 hours.
3 minAgree the next step and a decision date.

When you win

  1. Mark the lead Won in the app and enter the final deal value — this adds it to your Monthly business.
  2. Pass the details to the DTA team for setup; follow your manager’s payment process.
  3. Check in on day 7 (is everything working?) and day 30 (results, feedback), and ask for a referral.
If you lose a deal, mark it Lost and write the reason in Notes (price, timing, competitor, no need). Looking at your lost reasons each month is the fastest way to improve.

Stages 4 & 5 · Months 4–12 — Momentum and growth

Months 4–6: build momentum

  • Ask for referrals after every good result: “Which two businesses you know have the same problem?” Referrals close faster than cold visits.
  • Revisit your best segments. Look at which kinds of businesses became clients, and visit more like them.
  • Review yourself once a month using My dashboard and Monthly business: leads → conversations → demos → clients. Which step is weakest? Work on that one thing next month.
  • Share good results (with the client’s permission) with new prospects — a real example convinces better than any brochure.

Months 7–12: grow and lead

  • Keep clients renewing. Remind them 2–3 weeks before the renewal date, fix any problem first, and set the follow-up date in the app.
  • Offer add-ons. A software client may need a website or app. Ask after they have used the product for 30–60 days.
  • Help new teammates. Share your scripts and what worked. A strong team makes every state stronger.
  • Look at your year. In Monthly business, switch to Financial year (Apr–Mar) to see your total business and best month, then set your goals for the next year with your manager.

Your toolkit

Answers to common objections

Listen fully, agree with the worry, answer with facts, then ask for the next step. Never argue.

They sayYou can say
“It is too expensive.”“I understand. Let’s compare the monthly cost with the time and mistakes you have today.” Show the saving first; offer only the price options your manager has approved.
“We already use Excel / another software.”“Good — what is the one thing it doesn’t do well? Let me show how we handle that.”
“My staff won’t learn it.”“We train your team and support them after the start. We’ll begin with the daily tasks only.”
“I need to think / ask my partner.”“Of course. What would you like to be clear on? Can we speak again on ___ with your partner?”
“Send details on WhatsApp.”Send a short, specific message and fix a call time: “I’ll call you Thursday at 5.”
“Is my data safe?”Explain only what your manager has confirmed. If unsure: “Let me confirm and get back to you today.”

Do

  • Log every lead and activity the same day.
  • Ask questions before pitching.
  • Be on time for demos and calls.
  • Follow up on the dates you set.
  • Be honest about features and timelines.
  • Ask happy clients for referrals.

Don’t

  • Don’t promise features or dates you can’t confirm.
  • Don’t quote prices or discounts outside the approved list.
  • Don’t share your login with anyone.
  • Don’t criticise competitors — show your value.
  • Don’t leave a lead without a next follow-up date.
  • Don’t forget a client after the sale.
