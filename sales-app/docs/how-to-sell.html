How to Sell Project DTA

How to talk to customers, deal with them, and help them see why they need it

1. The big idea: people buy a solution, not a product

Nobody wakes up wanting “software”, “an app” or “a website”. They want less stress, fewer mistakes, more control, more customers and more profit. Your job is simple:

Find the customer’s real problem → help them see what it costs them → show how DTA solves it → make it easy to say yes.

Why customers feel they “need” it

A customer feels the need when they say out loud: “Yes, this is a problem, and it is costing me.” If you tell them, they argue. If you ask good questions and they discover it themselves, they buy. So ask more than you talk.

A deal in about 14 days

DayWhat happens

Day 1–3Discovery call (20 min) by phone or video call. Find the problem and its cost. Book the demo.

Small shops often decide faster; bigger or multi-partner businesses may take longer. What matters is that every step ends with a clear next step and a date.

Honest selling only. Creating “need” means helping the customer see a real problem — never inventing fear, fake deadlines or false promises. Customers who buy honestly stay, renew and refer others.

2. How to communicate

The first minute decides the meeting

  • Be ready and on time. Greet warmly, smile, say the customer’s name. Use “Sir / Madam / Ji” respectfully.
  • Speak their language — Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Malayalam or whatever they are comfortable in. Mix in English words only if they do.
  • Be short. Introduce yourself in one breath, then hand the conversation to them with a question.
Say: “Namaste ji, I’m ___ from DTA. We help businesses like yours ___ . I’m not here to push anything — I’d like to understand how you manage ___ today. Do you have 5 minutes?”

The 70 / 30 rule

The customer should talk about 70% of the time. You talk 30% — mostly questions. The more they talk, the more they trust you and the more they tell you what they need.

Phone and WhatsApp

  • Call at a sensible time (avoid lunch and closing hours). Ask “Is this a good time for 2 minutes?”
  • WhatsApp: short, one idea per message, their name first, no long brochures. End with one question or one next step.
  • Reply within an hour during working hours. Speed shows you care.

3. Making them feel the need — step by step

Step A · Discover: questions that open the problem

Pick 4–6 questions that fit the business. Listen, note, and ask a follow-up to each answer.

AreaQuestions to ask
Today’s way“How do you handle billing / stock / orders / customers right now?” · “Who does it and how long does it take?”
Time“How many hours a week go on this? What would you do with that time?”
Mistakes & money“Have you ever lost money from a wrong bill, missed payment or stock mismatch? How often?”
Control“Can you see today’s sales and pending payments instantly, or do you have to ask someone?”
Customers & growth“How do new customers find you? Do you lose customers because they can’t reach you or order easily?”
Staff & future“What happens when a key person is absent? Where do you want the business in 2–3 years — and what is stopping that?”

Step B · Show the cost of staying the same

Help them put a number on the problem — with their own figures.

Example (illustrative numbers — always use the customer’s own):
“You said billing and checking takes about 2 hours a day. That is about 50 hours a month. If an hour of your or your staff’s time is worth ₹150, that is about ₹7,500 a month — before counting the mistakes you mentioned. Does that sound right?”
Let them confirm the numbers. When the customer says the figure, it becomes true for them.

Step C · Paint the “after” picture

“Imagine closing your day in 10 minutes, seeing every pending payment on one screen, and your staff making fewer mistakes. If we could get you there, how would that change your week?”

Step D · Prove it

  • Show, don’t tell: a live demo using their kind of data.
  • Real examples: “A trader in ___ was facing the same issue; now he ___” (only true stories, with permission). Ask your manager for approved examples
  • Make it low-risk: explain support, training and what happens after the sale.
Honest reasons to act now — use only real ones: the cost of each month they wait; a busy or filing season coming; a start slot your team can really offer; an offer your manager has really approved, with a real end date.

Handling any objection — 4 steps (LAER)

  1. Listen fully without interrupting.
  2. Acknowledge: “That’s a fair concern.”
  3. Explore: “What exactly worries you about it?” (the real reason is often different)
  4. Respond with a fact, an example or a demo — then ask: “Does that answer your concern?”
They sayTry this
“It’s too expensive.”“Compared to what? Let’s compare it with the ₹___ a month you told me the problem costs.”
“We already manage fine.”“Glad it works. What’s the one thing you wish was easier? Let me show just that.”
“I’ll think about it.”“Of course. What exactly would you like to think over — price, timing or how it works? I’ll help you with it.”
“Is it safe? What if it stops?”Answer only what DTA has confirmed; if unsure: “Let me confirm and tell you today.” Then do it.
“Come back later.”“Sure. When exactly should I call? And what should we have decided by then?” — set a date in the app.

5. Scripts you can use

First call (about 1 minute)

“Namaste ___ ji, this is ___ from DTA. We help [type of business] in [city] to [save time on billing / get more customers online]. I’d like to ask two quick questions — is this a good time? … How are you handling [billing] today? … I see. Could we speak for 15 minutes on [day], by phone or video call, so I can understand it better and show you something that may help?”

Discovery call (20 minutes)

“Thank you for your time. Today I mainly want to listen. Could you walk me through how a normal day goes? … [ask 4–6 questions from section 3, take notes] … So if I understand: [summarise their 2–3 problems and the cost]. Is that right? … If we could fix that, would it be worth looking at? Let me show you on [day] — who else should join?”

Demo (25 minutes)

“Last time you told me [problem 1] and [problem 2]. I’ll show only how we handle those. … [show 3 things] … How would this work for your staff? … What would you want to see changed?”

Closing — pick the one that fits

Direct: “It fits what you told me. Shall we start next Monday?”
Choice: “Would you prefer to start with the monthly plan or the yearly plan?” (only approved plans)
Small step: “Let’s set up your first 20 items together this week so you can see it with your own data.”
Next step: “What do you need to decide — a talk with your partner? Can we meet them on Thursday?”

WhatsApp messages

After first call: “Thank you for your time today, ___ ji. As discussed on our call, your main concerns are [1] and [2]. I’ll show you how we handle them on [day/time]. Is that fine?”
After demo: “Thank you ___ ji. Summary: [problem] → [what DTA does] → [price / plan] → start on [date]. Shall I confirm? I’ll call you [day] at [time].”
No reply for a few days: “Hello ___ ji, just checking if you had a chance to think. If anything is unclear, I’m happy to help — would [day] work for a 5-minute call?”

6. After the meeting: close, follow up, keep the client

If they say YES

  1. Thank them. Repeat what you agreed: product, price, start date, who will set it up.
  2. In the DTA Sales app, mark the lead Won and enter the final deal value.
  3. Hand over to the DTA team; follow your manager’s payment process.
  4. Call after 7 days (“Is everything working?”) and 30 days (“What has improved?”). Then ask: “Who else do you know who has this problem?”

If they say NOT YET

  1. Find the real reason (price, timing, trust, partner, no need).
  2. Agree a date for the next conversation and set the follow-up in the app.
  3. Between calls send something useful (a tip, a short video, a similar client’s result) — not just “any update?”.

If they say NO

  1. Stay polite: “Thank you for your honesty. May I ask what made the difference?”
  2. Mark Lost with the reason in Notes — you learn from it.
  3. Ask for a referral or stay in touch; people often return.
